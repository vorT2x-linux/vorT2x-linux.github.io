One app for your T2 Mac

Kirk25 November 20264 min read

Until now, VorT2x put about a dozen apps in your app menu: Fan Control, Zone Power, Power Explorer, Power Tune, SMC Control, GPU Control, Force Click, TrueTone, AirShare, T2 Debug and VorT2x Update. Each had its own window, its own icon and its own idea of where things go.

In VorT2x v0.3.0 they're one app: the VorT2x Dashboard. One window, a sidebar on the left, and a page for every part of your Mac that VorT2x set up.

Only what your Mac has

T2 Macs are not all the same. A MacBook Pro has a Touch Bar, a Force Touch trackpad and a battery. An iMac Pro has none of those, but it has a big Vega GPU and two fans to keep it cool. The installer already knows this and only sets up what each Mac needs.

The dashboard simply follows the installer. A page appears when its part is installed, and not otherwise. On my iMac Pro that's Fans, Zone Power, Power Explorer, Power Tune, SMC, AirShare, Debug and Updates. A MacBook also gets Force Click, the MacBook Pros with a switchable AMD graphics card get Graphics, and Macs with an ambient light sensor running GNOME get TrueTone. There's no list to maintain and no hardware check done twice: the installer decides once, and the dashboard shows the result.

What it looks like

The sidebar sits right on the window background, and the page you pick floats in a rounded card next to it. Each page keeps its own controls in its header, so the fan switch, the refresh button or the RTC sync button are where you'd expect them. At the bottom of the sidebar, a small card tells you which Mac you're on and what it runs, for example iMac Pro (2017), Fedora 45 · COSMIC. Light and dark themes both work, and the icons are built into the app, so they look right on GNOME and COSMIC alike.

Lighter on your Mac

A dashboard with eleven pages could easily be eleven apps running at once. It isn't:

  • Pages are built the first time you open them. Opening the dashboard doesn't check GitHub, ask for your password or start reading sensors until you go to the page that needs it.
  • Pages that update live only do so while you're looking. Fans, Zone Power, SMC, Graphics and TrueTone pause their readings when you switch to another page. Graphics used to ask for root access every three seconds and TrueTone used to check its service every second, even in the background.
  • The work underneath hasn't changed. The fan daemon, the power daemon, the SMC service and the rest keep running exactly as before. Only the windows moved.

Power Tune, rewritten

One app couldn't simply move in. Power Tune, which tunes PCIe link power states, runtime power management and wake sources while showing how deep your CPU sleeps, was written in Python. The rest of VorT2x is Rust, so I rewrote its window in Rust to make it a page like the others. The parts that do the actual work as root are the same as before, so your saved settings and the service that restores them at boot carry on as they were.

Getting it

The dashboard is in VorT2x v0.3.0. Already running VorT2x? Open VorT2x Update one last time: after the update it's the VorT2x Dashboard in your app menu, and the separate apps are gone from it. New installs get the dashboard straight away. All the details are in the pull request.

I've tested it on an iMac Pro. Force Click, Graphics and TrueTone don't appear on mine, so those three pages haven't been opened on a Mac that has them yet. If yours does, tell us how they look.

And if you like where this is going, consider supporting VorT2x on Patreon. It pays for test hardware, and maybe one day for a MacBook to test these pages on.
