Check my Mac

Kirk27 November 20264 min read

"Does it work on my Mac?" is the first question anyone asks about Linux on a T2 Mac, and the honest answer has always been "it depends". There are sixteen T2 models, and the camera, the Touch Bar, Touch ID, the speakers and the 5K panel each depend on a different driver. Wiki pages go stale, and "works for me" from two years ago doesn't tell you much.

VorT2x v0.3.2 adds Check my Mac, a new first page in the VorT2x Dashboard. It looks for every T2 part on your Mac, tells you what works, and lets you send that to us in one click. Those reports will become a per-model list of what works on vort2x.org, built only from what real people saw on real Macs.

What it checks

Open the VorT2x Dashboard and Check my Mac is at the top of the sidebar. It runs straight away, needs no password and changes nothing on your Mac. It looks at:

  • The T2 bridge, the driver that everything else behind the T2 chip depends on.
  • Keyboard and trackpad, Touch Bar and Touch ID on MacBooks.
  • The FaceTime camera and ambient light sensor on MacBooks and iMacs.
  • Speakers and microphone, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, the SMC (fans and sensors) and graphics on every Mac.
  • The 5K display on the iMac Pro and the 2020 iMac: is the panel one 5120x2880 screen?
  • Sleep: has the Mac slept and woken this boot, and did the kernel log any errors on the way?

Each part shows as works, problem or not tried yet. VorT2x knows which parts each model has, so a Mac mini isn't told its camera is missing: parts your Mac doesn't have just say not on this Mac. Press the refresh button in the header to check again, for example after the Mac has been to sleep.

Sending a report

Below the results are two buttons:

  • Send report opens a new report on GitHub with your Mac model and the results already filled in. Add the things only you can tell, like whether sound actually plays or the camera shows a picture, and submit it.
  • Copy report puts the same text on your clipboard, if you'd rather email it or don't have a GitHub account.

Nothing leaves your Mac until you submit. The report holds only your model ID (like MacBookPro16,2), the Fedora, kernel and VorT2x versions, your desktop and the results. No serial number, host name, user name or network address. The page shows you every line before you send it.

Why it matters

VorT2x only claims something works once it's been tested on a real Mac. So far that's been my own iMac Pro and a 13-inch MacBook Pro. Every report from another model tells us something we couldn't find out on our own, and shows the next person with that Mac what they can expect before they install. A report that says "problem" is just as useful: it points us straight at the part to fix.

Once reports come in, vort2x.org will get a table of every T2 model and each part, with how many people confirmed it and on which release.

How it was checked

The rules for each model have their own automatic tests, and the whole Dashboard went through the project's static checks and a fresh build on Fedora 45. On real hardware, Check my Mac has been tried on my iMac Pro (2017), where every part shows as working. The MacBook checks (keyboard, Touch Bar, Touch ID) haven't run on a MacBook yet. If you have one, your report would be the first.

Getting it

Already running VorT2x? Open the Updates page in the VorT2x Dashboard and install v0.3.2, then open Check my Mac. New installs get it straight away. The full change is in the pull request.

Thank you to everyone who sends one in.
